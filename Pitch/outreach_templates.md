# Outreach Templates — Copy, Personalise, Send

Every template below is **a starting point, not a final draft.** Personalise the first two sentences; the structure can stay. Generic-feeling messages fail; specific ones convert.

The rule: **mention something only that one person would notice.** A book they recommended, a project they backed, a thing they said three years ago. That single detail turns a cold ask into a warm one.

---

## A. Tier-1 Inner Circle — ₹40K Ask (Phone or In-Person Only)

> **Never send Tier-1 asks by text.** Phone or in-person, always. Text after, to confirm the link, not to make the ask.

### Pre-call notes (write before the call)

- What is this person's current life context? (Don't ask for money during their crisis.)
- What is the most specific thing you can offer in return? (Acknowledgments page is generic. *"You will be in Chapter 8, which is set in [place they know]"* is specific.)
- Your ask number, said aloud, without flinching: *"₹40,000."*

### Phone-call script (skeleton)

```
[Catch up, 5 min, real, not performative.]

I want to tell you something. I'm writing the novel
this year — full time. The one I've been talking about
for [X years]. Sunday Cinemas optioned the film rights
in February.

I've put together a 90-day plan to raise $9,000 — about
₹7.5 lakhs — to make the writing year possible. I'm
asking a small circle of people I trust to sponsor a
chapter each. ₹40,000 per chapter.

Would you sponsor one?

[PAUSE. Let them respond. Do not fill the silence.]

[If yes:] Thank you. I'll send you the chapter list and
you can pick which one. Your name goes in the
acknowledgments under that chapter.

[If "let me think":] Of course. Can I call you back on
[specific day]? No pressure — I just don't want to chase
you.

[If no:] No problem. Would you forward the pitch to one
person you think might be interested? That helps me as
much as the money.
```

## B. Tier-2 Mid Circle — ₹10K Ask (Personal Email or Long WhatsApp)

```
Subject: A request — would you sponsor a chapter?

Hi [Name],

[One personal opening line — something specific to them,
recently. NOT "hope you're well."]

I'm writing my novel this year — *The President's Rule*,
a political fiction set in Karnataka. Sunday Cinemas
optioned the film rights last February for 24 months,
which gave me the push to commit to it full-time.

To make the writing year work, I've set a 90-day target
to raise $9,000 — about ₹7.5 lakhs. I'm asking a small
group of people I respect to sponsor a chapter each.

A chapter is ₹10,000. It covers the editorial pass and
one round of research for that chapter. In return:
  • Your name in the acknowledgments under that chapter
  • A signed copy on publication
  • Early access to chapters as they're drafted

If you'd like to read more before deciding, here's the
one-page pitch: [LINK]

If you're in, I'll send a payment link the same day.

Either way — thank you for reading this far.

Vishwa
+91 [number]
[substack URL]
```

**Why this works:**
- Personal opening line (one sentence, specific)
- Credibility lever (Sunday Cinemas) in paragraph 2
- Specific ask (₹10,000) with what it buys
- Three concrete deliverables
- Soft door (one-pager link) for the cautious
- Clear next step ("I'll send a payment link")

## D. Cold Ask — Warm-Intro-Style (When You Have a Mutual)

```
Subject: [Mutual's name] suggested I write to you

Hi [Name],

[Mutual] mentioned you read widely on Indian politics —
they thought you might be interested in a novel I'm
writing about a constitutional crisis in Karnataka.

The book is *The President's Rule*. Sunday Cinemas
optioned the film rights in February for 24 months. I'm
raising $9,000 across 90 days to write it full-time.

I'm not writing to ask for money in this first email.
I'm writing to ask if you'd be open to me sending the
one-page pitch and the first chapter — and if it
resonates, we could talk.

Either way, thank you for reading. [Mutual] speaks
highly of you.

Vishwa
[substack URL]
```

**Why no ask in the first email:** cold-from-mutual asks have higher conversion if you earn the conversation first. The pitch lands in email 2.

## F. Grant Cover Letter — IFA / Toto / Equivalent

```
[Date]

[Programme Officer Name]
[Org Name]
[Address]

Dear [Name],

I am writing to apply to [Programme Name] for support of
my debut novel, *The President's Rule*, a literary
political fiction set in Karnataka between 2024 and 2026.

The novel asks how an Indian state — and the people
inside it — behave when the federal architecture itself
is contested. It uses a Governor's invocation of
President's Rule as the dramatic spine for a deeper
inquiry into constitutional belonging, regional
identity, and the ethics of dissent.

The film rights were optioned by Sunday Cinemas in
February 2026 for 24 months, which I take as early
market validation of the project's reach.

I am requesting [SPECIFIC AMOUNT, e.g., ₹X lakhs] for
the [SPECIFIC LINE ITEM — e.g., editorial development
and field research]. A detailed budget is attached.

The enclosed proposal includes:
  • Synopsis (5 pages)
  • Sample chapter (Chapter 1, 4,200 words)
  • Author biography
  • Budget with line-item justifications
  • Timeline (90-day writing period)
  • Two references

I welcome the opportunity to discuss the work further.

Sincerely,
Shambhulingappa (Vishwa)
SWA Member No. 46108
[contact]
```

**Grant-specific notes:**
- Reference past awardees of the grant in the body if you can find a credible thread of relevance
- Always attach the budget as a clean separate PDF; never let it live inline
- Mention the Sunday Cinemas option in paragraph 3 *every time*

## G. Publisher / Agent Submission

```
Subject: Submission — THE PRESIDENT'S RULE — novel
       (optioned by Sunday Cinemas)

Dear [Agent / Editor's first name],

I am querying you with my debut novel, *The President's
Rule* — approximately 90,000 words, complete in first
draft, currently in revision.

When the Governor of Karnataka invokes President's Rule
on a constitutional pretext, [PROTAGONIST] must choose
between [STAKE A] and [STAKE B]. Set across Bangalore
and Delhi, the novel is in the tradition of [COMP A] and
[COMP B], with the urgency of a present-day political
moment.

The film rights are under option with Sunday Cinemas,
who signed a 24-month deal in February 2026.

I am a member of the Screenwriters Association of India
(No. 46108) and based in Bangalore.

Attached:
  • Synopsis (1 page)
  • Three chapters (first three)
  • Author biography

Full manuscript available on request.

Thank you for your time.

Vishwa
[contact]
```

**Why the Sunday Cinemas line is paragraph 3, not paragraph 1:** agents are allergic to feeling pitched-at. The story sells first; the option closes the deal.

## J. Follow-Up Templates

### After 5 days of silence (Email)

```
Hi [Name],

Just bumping this in case it got buried — no rush, but
wanted to make sure it landed.

[Original ask, in one sentence.]

If now isn't a good time, no problem — let me know and
I'll stop bothering you.

Vishwa
```

### After 10 days (last touch)

```
Hi [Name],

Last note from me on this — I respect your time.

If you'd like to support [chapter / patron tier / etc.],
the link is [LINK]. If not, that's completely fine and
I won't ask again.

Thank you for reading either way.

Vishwa
```

**Two follow-ups maximum.** Three follow-ups become harassment.

Three things from here:
  1. I'll send you the chapter list this week — pick

## Operating Principles

1. **One person per message.** Mass-bcc emails fail at 5× the rate of personal ones.
2. **The first line is everything.** If the first sentence is generic, the rest doesn't get read.
3. **Make the ask, then shut up.** The hardest skill is letting the silence after the ask do the work.
4. **Track everything.** Use [tracker.md](tracker.md). What gets measured gets sent.
5. **Send in batches but at variable times.** 5 sends Monday 10 AM, 5 Tuesday 4 PM, 5 Wednesday 8 PM. Some recipients only read at specific hours.
6. **Personalise the subject line.** "A request from Vishwa" beats "Supporting my novel" every time.
7. **You are not asking for charity.** You are offering a real product (the book) and a real position (named patron). Speak from that posture.
