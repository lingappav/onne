# Bangalore Leaders & Patrons — Weekend Outreach Plan

A focused contact framework for reaching the people in Bangalore who actually fund independent literary and cultural work — with a disciplined weekend cadence to work through them.

---

## ⚠️ A Strategic Caution to Read First

**The President's Rule is a political novel about constitutional collapse.**

That single fact reframes who you should and should not approach:

1. **Sitting / active politicians as personal patrons → avoid.** If they fund you and your book makes them look good, it reads as patronage. If it makes their party look bad, they won't fund anyway. Either outcome corrodes the novel's credibility. The cost is higher than the cheque.

2. **Politicians via institutional channels → fine.** MPLADS cultural-component applications, Karnataka Department of Kannada & Culture grants, Sahitya Akademi awards. These are *applications to offices*, not personal asks of politicians, and they leave no patronage shadow.

3. **Retired statesmen, ex-bureaucrats, judges → excellent.** They have the influence and standing but no current political stake. They are some of India's best private patrons of independent writing.

4. **Business leaders with cultural-philanthropy track records → the real engine.** Bangalore has one of the deepest concentrations of cultural philanthropists in India.

**This file is built around #2, #3, and #4. It deliberately does not generate a hit-list of sitting politicians to personally solicit.** That's a recommendation, not a limit — push back if you want to revisit.

## Category 4 — Retired Statesmen, Ex-Bureaucrats, Judges, Diplomats (Cultural Patrons)

These are often the most generous private patrons of serious writing in India. They have time, standing, and an interest in legacy projects. **Approach respectfully and with substance — they read carefully.**

Categories to identify in your network:
- **Retired IAS officers** of Karnataka cadre with literary interests
- **Retired High Court / Supreme Court judges** living in Bangalore
- **Retired diplomats** (IFS) with cultural roles post-retirement
- **Former Vice-Chancellors** of Karnataka universities
- **Senior journalists turned writers** (some genuinely fund younger writers; ask warmly)

**How to find them:** ask three trusted seniors in your network — *"Who in your generation cares about new Kannada writing in English?"* — and follow the names.

## Category 5 — Politicians: Institutional Channels Only

This is the section where the framing in §0 matters most.

### Institutional paths (legitimate, applicable)

| Channel | What | How to access |
|---|---|---|
| **MPLADS — cultural component** | Each MP has a Local Area Development Fund (~₹5 Cr/year). Cultural projects sometimes funded. | Formal written proposal to MP's office secretariat — for a *project* (e.g., "Bangalore reading event series"), not personal advance |
| **MLALADS — Karnataka MLAs' funds** | State-level equivalent | Same process, lower cap |
| **Department of Kannada & Culture (GoK)** | State ministry grants | Annual application cycle; published guidelines |
| **Karnataka Sahitya Akademi grants** | Direct publication subsidy | Application-based |
| **Karnataka Nataka Akademi / Lalithakala Akademi** | Adjacent if your work crosses to theatre/visual | Application-based |

### Cultural-political bridge figures (sometimes useful, always institutional)

People who are *politically connected but operate via cultural roles* — e.g., chairpersons of cultural bodies, advisors to ministries, board members of state cultural institutions. These are public-facing roles, accessible through their institution's office.

**Rule of engagement:** approach the office and the institutional role, not the personal political identity. Always.

### What to never do

- Do not cold-DM or call a sitting MLA / MP personally to ask for a contribution to your novel.
- Do not accept undocumented cash. Period.
- Do not let any single political contribution exceed 5% of your total raise. Concentration = leverage = compromise.
- Do not include any contributor's name in acknowledgments without their written consent.

## The Weekend Outreach Cadence (12 Weekends × 4 Hours)

The work-week is for writing the book. **The weekend is when you reach leaders.**

A 4-hour Saturday block + a 2-hour Sunday block, every weekend for 12 weekends = 72 hours of focused outreach. That is enough to systematically work through the categories above.

### Each weekend has the same shape:

```
SATURDAY MORNING (10:00 – 12:30)  •  RESEARCH BLOCK
   - 90 min: research 5 new prospects (read what they fund, what
     they write, what they care about — be specific)
   - 60 min: write 5 personalised first-touch messages
   - These are NOT sent today

SATURDAY AFTERNOON (15:00 – 16:30)  •  CULTIVATION BLOCK
   - Attend one cultural event in Bangalore (BIC talk, Atta
     Galatta reading, Suchitra screening, Sahitya Parishat meet)
   - Goal: meet 2 new people in person, exchange cards / numbers
   - Add them to the pipeline on Sunday

SUNDAY MORNING (10:00 – 11:30)  •  OUTREACH BLOCK
   - 30 min: review and polish the 5 messages drafted Saturday
   - 30 min: send 5 first-touch messages
   - 30 min: send follow-ups to anyone at +5 days, +10 days
   - Update tracker.md

SUNDAY EVENING (18:00 – 18:30)  •  REPLY BLOCK
   - Reply to every email / DM that came in this weekend
   - Personal, hand-written, never templated for the reply
```

**Total weekly outreach output:**
- 5 new asks sent
- 5 follow-ups sent
- 2 new in-person introductions
- 100% inbox cleared

Over 12 weekends: **60 new asks, 24 in-person meetings, ~120 follow-ups.** At a realistic 20% conversion, that's **12 yeses** — combined with the weekday Substack / patron stream, this hits the $9K target.

## The Three-Pile Sort (do this Sunday Evening of every weekend)

Take every new contact made this weekend and put them in one of three piles:

| Pile | Definition | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **PILE A** — Likely patron / introducer | Engaged, asked questions, gave you their card unprompted | Personalised follow-up within 48 hrs + send one-pager |
| **PILE B** — Polite but uncertain | Took your card but didn't volunteer theirs | One follow-up only, with a low-friction ask ("would you read 5 pages?") |
| **PILE C** — Not a fit | No real engagement | No follow-up. Don't burn time on no-fits. |

Discipline on this sort is what makes the cadence sustainable. Without it, the pipeline bloats and the real prospects get the same attention as the no-shots.

## The Single Hardest Sentence to Internalise

> *Most Bangalore leaders will not fund your novel. The ones who will, will fund it generously. Your job is to find the small number, not to convince the large number.*

This reframes everything:
- 5 yeses out of 60 asks isn't failure. It is the entire game.
- An hour spent on a B-pile contact is an hour stolen from a different A-pile contact you haven't met yet.
- The 12-weekend grind exists to *surface* the small number — not to convert the large one.

## What This File Is Not

It is not a list of named politicians to ask for money. It is deliberately not that.

If at some point you want to revisit that decision — say, for an *institutional* MPLADS application to a specific MP whose constituency or interests align — that is a separate, formal, written-record-only conversation. The file structure to handle it is already in place under Category 5 above.

But for the 90-day fundraise, the path is:
**foundations + family offices + retired patrons + cultural-establishment + institutional channels.** That is where the $9,000 lives.
