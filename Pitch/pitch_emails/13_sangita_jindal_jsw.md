I am Shambhulingappa (Vishwa), a Bangalore-based novelist

I write because the novel's deepest research strands are
not the policy chapters but the philosophical ones — and
those are the strands I imagine you might find most worth
engaging with:

  • Kautilya's Dandaneeti as the spine of the novel.
    Every act of state power in the book is implicitly
    measured against the Kautilyan calibration of
    *tikshna danda* (excess), *mridu danda* (laxness),
    and *yathaarha danda* (just punishment). I have
    drafted an 11-part scholarly manual mapping the
    Arthashastra's Books 3 and 4 to modern Indian
    governance — that manual is the substrate; the
    novel is the translation into character.

  • Dharma-justified revenge — the ethics of retribution
    in Indian thought. The novel asks the Mahabharata's
    central question of every major character: when does
    private hurt become legitimate retribution? Drawing
    on Krishna's dharma-yuddha argument, the four
    upayas (Sama, Dana, Bheda, Danda), and B.K.
    Matilal's *Ethics and Epics*, the chapter is a
    serious philosophical engagement — not a polemic.
    The point of the chapter is the *difficulty* of
    dharma, not its triumphalist invocation.

The novel does not write essays. It dramatises. But the
research substrate is rigorous, and I think it earns the
kind of literary patronage JSW Foundation has long
extended to serious Indian work.

The full 90-day independent fundraise target is $9,000.
I would welcome the JSW Foundation's consideration of
supporting the editorial pass on these two philosophical
chapters (~$1,500), or — if of interest — a more
substantial sponsorship of the philosophical-substrate
work that underlies the book.

I would equally value the chance to send the first
chapter and the Dandaneeti manual excerpt for your read,
ahead of any formal ask. Your engagement with the work
would itself be patronage.
