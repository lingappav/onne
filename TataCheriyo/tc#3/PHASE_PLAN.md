# tc#3 — Phase 3: LAND THE DREAM

> "Now they belong to the banner. Phase 3 is where the audience stops being audience and starts being part of the story."

**Release Day:** Day 115
**Phase Window:** Day 21 (parallel pre-prod begins) – Day 120 (slate-wrap event)
**Audience Goal:** Identification. The city should be saying, *"I am a TataCheriyo person. I was there for tc#1."*

---

## 1. Phase Mission

This is the **biggest, most ambitious, most public** film of the slate. Everything tc#1 planted and tc#2 deepened pays off here. Phase 3 must feel like a **culmination**, not just a release.

**Brand objective:** land the dream. Convert a built audience into a community.
**Commercial objective:** clean profit theatrically. OTT and satellite as cream.
**Cultural objective:** the slate becomes a case study. Other indie producers in Bangalore ask, *"How did they do that?"*

---

## 2. Format

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| **Runtime** | 100–120 minutes |
| **Genre** | The flagship piece — the most resonant, most emotionally complete film of the three. Drama with scale, OR drama with one big idea, OR a piece that synthesises the tones of tc#1 (intimate) and tc#2 (ensemble) into something larger. |
| **Language** | Kannada primary. Pan-India OTT-ready. |
| **Locations** | 10–14 total. Bangalore's lesser-shot textures (industrial, infrastructural, peripheral). |
| **Cast size** | 10–15 principal speaking roles. Cameo space for tc#1 and tc#2 faces. |
| **VFX** | Selective but visible — 2–3 set pieces |
| **Music** | Full score + 3 songs. The TataCheriyo motif is now the audience's anthem — used with confidence. |
| **Budget tier** | ~₹1.62 Cr total |

**Concept sweet spot:** the film that **only TataCheriyo could have made** — because it requires the universe and audience built across tc#1 and tc#2 to land its biggest beats.

## 5. Casting Strategy

- **One bona-fide star or major name** — for the first time in the slate. The audience-build of tc#1 and tc#2 means the star is now joining *us*, not the other way around. Negotiate on those terms.
- **Cameos** from tc#1 and tc#2 principal cast — even one-scene appearances. Universe payoff.
- **Open audition** for one role, publicised — the *"someone from the audience could be in the film"* gesture that closes the brand loop.
- **A single newcomer in a pivotal role** — keeps the discovery DNA alive.

## 7. The Universe Payoff

Phase 3 is where the shared-world hooks planted in tc#2 (and seeded in tc#1) **resolve**.

- A character from tc#1 returns — older, changed, the audience gasps
- The location used in both tc#1 and tc#2 is the site of tc#3's climax
- The TataCheriyo sonic motif, used sparingly in tc#1 and confidently in tc#2, plays in **full** at tc#3's ending — the audience knows it before it lands
- The thematic question (asked in tc#1, circled in tc#2) gets its answer — or, better, gets a fuller question

**The triple-bill on Day 120 makes all of this legible at once.** That is when the slate becomes a *trilogy in the social mind* — even though it was never marketed as one.

## 9. Risks Specific to Phase 3

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Ambition exceeds capacity in the final sprint | Two-unit shoot is the only scale concession. Don't add a third unit. |
| Crew burnout in days 95–115 | Bring in 2–3 specialist hires (additional editor assistant, additional sound assistant) for the final crunch only |
| Star delays push schedule | Star contracted with a hard window. Substitution plan rehearsed for one lead. |
| Censor friction on a bigger / bolder film | File earlier (Day 102). Have an appellate plan ready. |
| Slate-wrap event flops because it's logistics-heavy | Dedicated event producer hired Day 90. Not the film team's responsibility. |
| Anchor multiplex tries to renegotiate the slot | Slate deal from Day 1 locks Day 115 contractually |
| Overconfidence — the brand starts believing its own legend and gets sloppy on craft | Treat tc#3 with **more** discipline than tc#1, not less. The dream is landed on craft, not hype. |
