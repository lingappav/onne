Week 1 — Day by Day Plan (May 20–26)

This week is entirely research — no prose writing yet. The goal is to build a rock-solid knowledge base so every chapter writes itself with specific, accurate, vivid detail.

Day
Task Focus (Morning)
Task Focus (Evening) + Daily Goal
Day 1
May 20
Read all source documents end to end
Re-read Master Narrative, Table of Contents, all PDF chapters (English_Chapters, Sampath Kumar, The President's Rule). Annotate gaps, inconsistencies, and unanswered questions.
Create a "Research Gap List"
Write 1–2 pages of specific questions: What really happened in the 2023–2024 Karnataka elections? What does Article 356 actually say? What was the actual ONOE timeline? List 20 questions to answer this week.
Day 2
May 21
Constitutional Research — Article 356 & President's Rule
Study: S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994 Supreme Court judgment). Real cases: Arunachal Pradesh 2016, Maharashtra 2019. How are governors involved? What exactly does a Special Administrator do vs. an elected CM?
Write a 2-page "Legal Framework Note"
This becomes a reference for every political scene. Include: powers of the administrator, limitations, judicial review history, Delhi's levers of control. Directly feeds Ch 1, 2, 9, 16.
Day 3
May 22
Karnataka Political Landscape Research
Study: Coalition dynamics 2023–2025, JD(S), INC, BJP power plays. Karnataka's black economy in real estate. Actual stray dog crisis data in Bengaluru. Alcohol policy battles. Research the BBMP & mid-day meal programme.
Build a "World Bible" document
Ground the novel in real Bengaluru geography: Vidhana Soudha, Cubbon Park, Shivajinagar, Kodagu. Real newspaper headlines, real Twitter hashtag patterns from the period. Makes prose feel lived-in, not fictional.
Day 4
May 23
Character Bible — Deep Dive
Write full profiles for: (1) Dr. Sampath Kumar — backstory, oncology specialty, Ranchi life, marriage, motivations. (2) Priyadarshini — her own agency, sacrifices. (3) The Architect — which real-world politician archetype? (4) Jyestha, Prabhakar, Kavitha.
One scene exercise per character
Write 200 words from each character's POV — not for the novel, just to find their voice. Sampath thinks like a diagnostician. The Architect thinks in poker. Jyestha thinks in patience. This unlocks authentic dialogue for all chapters.
Day 5
May 24
ONOE Research + Thematic Framework
One Nation One Election: what is the actual policy, who supports/opposes it, what is the constitutional amendment required? How does the novel position ONOE — as a good idea misused, or a bad idea? Nail the thematic stance.
Write the Novel's "Spine Statement"
One paragraph: what this novel is arguing about democracy. "Not that President's Rule is good, but that…" This becomes the compass for every editorial decision in the draft. Also drafts the back-cover blurb you need for pitches.
Day 6
May 25
Beat Sheet — Act I (Ch 1–8)
For each of the 8 Act I chapters: write a 5-line beat sheet. Scene — Opening image → Conflict → Turn → Decision → Closing image. Lock every key line of dialogue (from Master Narrative). Note word count target per chapter (aim 2,500–3,500 words each).
Week 1 Review + Plan Week 2
Read through all notes. Identify: What did you NOT know that you now know? What is still unclear? Update Day 8–14 plan. Send one email to a Karnataka-based political journalist — introduce yourself, request 30-min call for background research.
Day 7
May 26
Rest + Inspiration Day
Read one comparable novel: "The Space Between Us" (Thrity Umrigar) OR rewatch Nayakan / A Wednesday — films that show how lone integrity plays in Indian political contexts. Note what makes them work cinematically.
Visualize Chapter 1 as a film opening
Write 300 words describing Ch 1 as a film director would shoot it: camera angles, sound design, the moment the SUVs arrive at Ranchi. This is pre-writing that makes Ch 1 prose vivid. You start writing Chapter 1 prose on Day 15. That is next week.
Week 1 Success Criteria: By end of Day 7 you have — (1) Research Gap List answered, (2) Legal Framework Note, (3) World Bible draft, (4) Character Bible with 6 profiles, (5) Spine Statement written, (6) 8 Act I beat sheets. You are 100% ready to write prose from Day 15.

Risks & Blockers

Know these now so they don't surprise you at Day 50.

Risk 1 — Writing pace falling behind (most likely)
2,500 words/day is ambitious alongside life. Mitigation: set a weekly word count not daily. 17,500 words per week allows for 2 rest days. If Act I is late by a week, compress Act II research (it's already beat-sheeted in the Master Narrative).
Risk 2 — Sunday Cinemas option lapses (24-month window from Feb 2026)
The option expires ~Feb 2028. You have time. But deliver the first draft by Jul 18 to trigger the advance conversation before the window is half-done. Don't let silence become a gap.
Risk 3 — Superstar non-response
Cold outreach to celebrities has ~5% response rate. Mitigation: identify warm introductions for each name (through Hombale connection, through Malavika Avinash's lawyer network, through Thaara's political contacts). Never cold-email directly — find a mutual name.
Risk 4 — Political sensitivity of the ONOE framing
The novel is fictional but the ONOE link is real (per your Appendix B). If the political climate shifts, the funding framing may need adjustment. Keep the pitch flexible: "democracy story" first, "ONOE" only with aligned partners.
Risk 5 — Prabhakar's story needs careful legal review
A character who is killed by a system connected to a sitting MP is fiction — but make sure it is unambiguously fictional and no real person can claim defamation. Schedule a 1-hour legal review of the Prabhakar chapters before submission (Day 83).
