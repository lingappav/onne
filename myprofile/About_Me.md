## 4. Long-form About Me (≈ 600 words) — for the Substack "About" page, personal website, long-form pitch

> ### About
>
> My name is Shambhulingappa. People who know me call me Vishwa.
>
> I was born in Karnataka and trained as an engineer. For fifteen years I have built software for companies whose names everyone knows — Mindtree, American Express, Dell, Fidelity Investments — across India and the United States. I have led teams, architected distributed systems used by financial-services clients around the world, and learned the long, quiet patience that enterprise code teaches you. That part of my life is on a separate resume; this page is about the other part.
>
> Because that has never been the whole story.
>
> In the early 2010s, I was roaming around Karnataka with a script in my head and a producer-director almost ready to take it on. Just before it could begin, America called, and I went. I did not like America. So in October 2017 I came back, this time to try filmmaking properly, and ended up working on two Kannada films — ***Iruvudellava Bittu*** and ***Hondisi Bareyiri***. Both of those experiences taught me what cinema actually asks of a person: not effort, but the willingness to give it a decade.
>
> I gave what I could. Then I took a break from the hustle and came back to America.
>
> What I have learned since is that I cannot sit still without writing. The stories that wanted out then still want out now. So this time — instead of chasing producers or building a career on top of a career — I am simply telling them. All of them. To end this loop, finally, by finishing what I have been starting to say for a decade and a half.
>
> I do not write to make money. If anything, whatever this work earns back I am willing to give in loyalty to the family that has carried me through every back-and-forth of the last decade. What I want from writing is something quieter — to leave behind a record of a particular Karnataka, a particular generation, and a particular way of seeing the country we live in.
>
> I describe myself as a **storytelling enthusiast** — by which I mean someone who keeps stumbling on perspectives that no one seems to be writing about, and who has finally decided to be the one to write them.
>
> The novel I am writing is called ***The President's Rule***. It is a literary political fiction set in Karnataka between 2024 and 2026, built on six research strands — Kautilya's Dandaneeti, the architecture of media trials in India, the ethics of dharma-justified retribution, the civic distinctiveness of Karnataka, the federalism question beneath One Nation One Election, and the long history of political crimes committed in India without any technology at all. Sunday Cinemas optioned the film rights in February 2026 for a twenty-four-month period. I am a member of the Screenwriters Association of India (No. 46108).
>
> The chapters will appear weekly on Substack across the writing year. If the work speaks to you, the support page explains how patrons make it possible. If you are simply a reader who has come this far — thank you. The book is being written for you.

## 6. The Single Sentence You Should Be Able to Say in an Elevator

> *"I am a Karnataka-born novelist with fifteen years in enterprise software, two Kannada films behind me, and a political novel under option with Sunday Cinemas — I am raising independently to write it full-time for ninety days."*

This is the **calling card sentence.** It does five things at once: roots you in Karnataka, gives you weight (engineering + cinema), names the work, signals credibility (option), and names the ask (fundraise). Workshop it until it sounds like you, in your voice.

---

## Notes on the Narrative Voice

Three principles run through every version of the bio above:

1. **The engineer is not hidden, but the engineer is not the lead either.** Mention it, move on. The novelist is the lead.
2. **The films are named.** *Iruvudellava Bittu* and *Hondisi Bareyiri* are real, recognisable Kannada films — naming them puts you in a tradition rather than in the abstract category "tried filmmaking."
3. **The honesty of the back-and-forth is the strongest line, not a weakness.** "I did not like America. I came back. I tried. I came back again. Now I write." That sentence is more compelling than any straightened-out story would be. The reader trusts the writer who tells the truth about their own restlessness.
