flag Sunday Cinemas · Mission Statement
"The most expensive mistake in Indian cinema is taking a half-baked idea to a director's table. We fix that problem at the source — by finishing the story first."
— Ramenahalli Jagannatha, Director & Founder, Sunday Cinemas

Sunday Cinemas was built on a single conviction: the writer is the most underprotected creative in Indian film. Scripts get rewritten by producers, diluted by stars, mutilated by committees. Stories die in development. The writer loses credit, rights, and sometimes the will to create again.

Ramenahalli Jagannatha — producer of two Kannada feature films and director of Hondisi Bareyiri, Teerataroopa Tandeyavarge, and Tamma Sukhagamana Bayasuva — built Sunday Cinemas to reverse this. He has personally adopted The President's Rule and will direct it himself, mentoring Vishwa Shambhulingappa autonomously through the writing process and guiding the research team every step of the way.

The model is simple: finish the novel first, at the highest literary standard possible, before a single frame is planned. Once the manuscript is complete, Ramenahalli brings in experienced screenplay writers to adapt it into film language — ensuring the political authenticity of the novel translates into box-office scale. The writer owns the work. The director owns the vision. Nobody compromises.

The President's Rule is the first novel in this initiative. What it builds — the community, the credibility, the proof-of-concept — becomes the platform for every writer who comes after.

The Giants Have Already Proven the Ceiling

We are not guessing at the upside. The path from a single morally complex protagonist to a global audience has been walked before — and the films that walked it are some of the most successful stories of the modern era. The White Tiger turned a Booker Prize into a Netflix global release, proving that literary awards accelerate OTT acquisition. Gangubai Kathiawadi took book-first source material to ₹209 Crore at the box office, the Bhansali template for literary adaptation at scale.

The genre comparison sits even closer. The Kerala Story crossed ₹290 Crore as politically charged, content-driven cinema carried by word of mouth rather than a star — exactly the lane The President's Rule occupies. Add a superstar to that lane and you reach Sarkar, the ₹250 Crore-plus political drama that marks the upper ceiling when this novel has its A-list cast moment.

And the prestige ceiling is just as real. Wolf Hall showed that the administrative-procedural political drama is premium global OTT content, while All the King's Men — Pulitzer Prize, three film adaptations — is the literary ancestor of this story. Dr. Sampath Kumar is its photographic negative: the man who refuses everything Willie Stark embraced. The ceiling isn't a hope. The giants have already built it.
